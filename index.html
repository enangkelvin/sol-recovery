Wallet compromised? Find what’s still there before it’s gone.

A free, read-only scan of your public address that surfaces leftover SOL, tokens and stake accounts, then walks you through securing them from a brand-new wallet.

We never ask for your seed phrase or private key, and nothing secret ever touches this site. Scanning needs only your public address. Securing assets uses your own wallet extension to sign, and you review every transaction there.

1. Scan your address

Read-only queries go straight from your browser to a Solana RPC node. Nothing is stored.

2. Secure assets (authority transfer)

This hands control of every asset account to a new safe address in one batch: token-account owner authority, stake and withdraw authority, and (optionally) the leftover SOL. After it lands, the attacker’s key no longer controls those assets. Your safe wallet pays the fees, so the compromised wallet needs no SOL and nothing is exposed to sweeper bots.


How it works: keep your safe account selected in Phantom and the compromised account selected in Solflare. Click Build and both wallets will prompt you to sign in sequence, then the transactions are sent automatically. Approve both prompts promptly, because blockhashes expire after roughly a minute; if that happens, use Retry. Remove the old account afterwards.

Why not “assign” the wallet to another program? A wallet address cannot itself become a token account. What changes is the authority on each asset account. Assigning the system account to a non-program address would lock its SOL forever, so this tool moves the SOL instead.

3. Action plan

  1. Assume the old seed phrase is burned. Create a brand-new wallet (preferably a hardware wallet) with a new seed phrase, generated on a clean device. Never import the old phrase into it.
  2. Move fast, but in the right order. Attackers often run “sweeper” bots that drain any SOL that lands in a compromised wallet. Don’t top up the old wallet with SOL for fees unless you’re ready to move everything in the same moment.
  3. Stake accounts: check who holds the withdraw authority (shown in the scan). If it is still your old key, you can deactivate and withdraw; if it was changed, those funds are controlled by someone else.
  4. Move highest-value assets first (staked SOL, then valuable tokens/NFTs), using a trusted wallet app or the Solana CLI, signing with the old key only as long as needed.
  5. Revoke token delegations and disconnect any malicious approvals on the old wallet.
  6. Find the root cause: scan your device for malware, review browser extensions, and rotate passwords and 2FA on exchange accounts.
  7. Report it: file with your local cybercrime authority (e.g. IC3 in the US, NPF-NCCC in Nigeria) and keep transaction signatures as evidence.

Honest note: if an attacker holds your key, they can race you for anything remaining. This tool cannot reverse past theft or freeze funds, and nobody legitimate can.

Recovery-scam alerts

“We can reverse the transaction”

Blockchain transactions are final. Anyone promising reversal is selling a scam.

Upfront fees

Fake recovery agents demand payment first. Real help (law enforcement, exchanges) never needs your crypto.

DMs after you post

Replies to your Telegram, X or Discord plea offering “help” are almost always impersonators.

“Connect wallet to validate”

Malicious sites trick you into signing drainer transactions. Verify before you ever sign.

FAQ

Can you get back what was already stolen?

No service can reverse on-chain transfers. You can report to exchanges if funds were deposited there, and to law enforcement. This tool focuses on securing what remains.

Why do you only need my public address?

Public addresses are visible to everyone on-chain. Reading balances, token accounts and stake accounts needs nothing secret.

What is “reclaimable rent”?

Each empty token account holds about 0.00204 SOL in rent that you can get back by closing the account.

Does this work for Token-2022 and liquid staking tokens?

Yes, both token programs are scanned. Liquid staking tokens (mSOL, JitoSOL, etc.) appear as ordinary token balances.
